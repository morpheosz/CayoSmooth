## What this is

Two parallel bodies of canon developed independently:

- **Repo canon** — `morpheosz/CayoSmooth` on `main`. Originated in Zach's
  Claude workshop. Deep on catalogue: all sixty-one titles, named personnel,
  four accessioned artifacts, runtimes, keys, catalog numbers, a built site.
- **Manna/GPT canon** — `Conversation_History_Manna_GPT_to_Codex_v0.1.md`.
  Originated in Chris's ChatGPT workshop. Deep on institution and music: the
  Restoration Initiative, the publication credit, real generated audio,
  Gil's Demo, the seal and tagline, merchandise.

The overlap is small, so most of this is a merge rather than a fight. Where
the two genuinely collide, Part A records the decision. Part B records new
questions the decisions created. Part C is blocked on assets.

## Ratification protocol

Per `CONTRIBUTING.md`, a canon change needs sign-off from a second
contributor and moves together with `CLAUDE.md`. This document is that
sign-off vehicle. Chris and Zach mark each item **RATIFIED** or **REJECTED**.
Items are independent — rejecting one does not block the others.

Nothing in Part A is executed until the item carries a ratification mark.

# Part A — Decisions

## A1. Expedition dates — the 1980/1981 dispute

**Repo:** 1979–1980, stated in `CLAUDE.md` and throughout the site.
**Manna/GPT:** 1979–1981, used as the display and merchandise range.
**Merch:** the concept shirt prints 1979–1981.

**DECISION (Zach, ratified by Chris):** Neither. The date range becomes the
Archive's most contested fact, and the contest is the content.

The documentary weight favours 1980. Charter, insurer's schedule, ledger,
and the boxing sequence of the reels are all consistent with a return in
1980, and that is the date the Archive uses in running prose. Against that
stands a single piece of physical evidence recovered at Grand Marais and
dated 1981, which the Archive has been unable to dismiss and unable to
explain. It has not revised the date. It has also not stopped arguing about
it.

This is the correct resolution because it converts a versioning accident
into the Archive's central open dispute, and it gives the Grand Marais
anomaly (A6) something to hang on.

**Consequences:**
- `CLAUDE.md` hard canon changes from a flat date to a dispute with a
  documented position: *the Archive dates the return to 1980; the Grand
  Marais evidence is dated 1981; the discrepancy is unresolved.*
- The shirt's "1979–1981" is not an error to correct. It is a Restoration
  Initiative fundraising item that took a side, and the Archive is aware of
  this. See A11.
- Requires a new artifact record. See B1.

**Files:** `CLAUDE.md`, `content/expedition/the-voyage.md`,
`content/expedition/the-recovery.md`, new artifact record, `SEED.md`.

## A3. Vocals — rotating vocalists, in the band and from the ports

**Repo:** Gil Vandermeer is "the credited vocalist on all sixty-one tracks."
**Manna/GPT:** silent on vocals.
**Problem:** the repo position is inconsistent with the way the recordings
actually sound, and it flattens sixty-one tracks onto one voice.

**DECISION:** Gil remains a vocalist and the most frequently credited one.
He is not the only one. The Expedition used a rotating series of singers —
some drawn from the four musicians, some picked up at ports of call and
recorded once.

The reason those singers were aboard is not musical. Vocalists were engaged
to settle obligations: bar tabs, dockage, a debt of a kind the ledger records
only as "settled ashore." Several appear on exactly one track and nowhere
else in any document the Archive holds.

**Why this is the right addition.** It is the single highest-value piece of
new canon in this reconciliation. It explains an observable property of the
recordings, it is entirely mundane, it generates artifacts rather than
resolving them, and it lets the ledger and the tape boxes disagree in a new
direction. The Archive's position should be procedural and slightly
embarrassed: vocalist attribution across the trove is incomplete, several
credits name a person who appears in no other document, and the Archive
considers the attribution project ongoing.

**Constraint:** no port vocalist gets a biography. Names may appear on tape
boxes. Nobody gets a dossier. This is a texture, not a cast.

**Consequences:**
- Gil's Demo (A12) still works. Its anomaly was never that Gil sang — it is
  that he played every instrument himself and performed the wrong sheet of
  paper.
- Frontmatter `personnel` blocks across released tracks need review, since
  they currently all lead with "Gil Vandermeer — vocals."

**Files:** `CLAUDE.md`, `content/personnel/gil-vandermeer.md`,
`content/personnel/roster.md`, new personnel record on vocalist attribution,
frontmatter on the five released tracks, `SEED.md`.

## A4. "Cabo Fever (Reprise)" — the illness framing is dropped

**Repo:** "The lyric refers to a period of illness contracted in Cabo."
**Manna/GPT:** a complete working lyric about a night at a harbour that
everyone remembers and nobody can place, with a spoken nautical-romance
breakdown.

**DECISION (Chris):** The illness framing does not survive. No yacht rock
band writes a song about being unwell. The reprise premise is untouched —
there is no original, there never was — but the catalogue entry must stop
describing an illness.

The replacement framing: the reprise refers to **an episode at Cabo**, never
described, which the song treats as common knowledge. The Archive records
that the episode is referenced and does not record what it was. This is
strictly stronger than "illness" — it preserves the confident-reference joke
without spending the mystery on a punchline.

**Preserved from the current entry:** the SLR-004a numbering gap and the
Archive's refusal to renumber. The "three place names, two proper nouns, and
one apparent inside joke about a customs official." The shortest-runtime
observation.

**Changed:** the illness sentence. And the claim that the charter logs do not
record the ketch visiting Cabo — see A11, which complicates this rather than
removing it.

**Lyric content is a separate work item.** The Manna/GPT working lyric is
strong and is not being adopted or rejected here. It needs a workshop pass
against the repo's established facts. That is follow-up, not part of this
merge.

**Files:** `content/discography/04-cabo-fever-reprise.md`, `SEED.md`.

## A5. "Chardonnay Diplomacy" — the audio is the artifact

**Repo:** 4:47, A major seventh, 84 BPM, "the lyric names neither party nor
the terms reached."
**Reality:** a generated master exists. It names the parties, it has a
chorus, and its runtime is not 4:47.

**DECISION:** The recording wins. The catalogue entry is amended.

Once audio ships, the entry is falsifiable by anyone who presses play, and an
archive caught contradicting its own published recording is a different and
much worse joke than the one intended.

The amendment is made **in-universe**, per `CONTRIBUTING.md`: the Archive
issues a correction on transfer. The original entry was prepared from the
splice log and the tape box before the reel was played end to end. This is
free, it is characterful, and it is the mechanism the repo already prescribes.

**Preserved:** the saxophone solo beginning at 2:14 and the tape ending
before the solo does. The "reel change, unavoidable" splice log entry. The
processional policy.

**BLOCKED** on the actual runtime and the selected master. See C1.

**Files:** `content/discography/02-chardonnay-diplomacy.md`, a correction
notice in `content/notices/`, `SEED.md`.

## A6. Grand Marais — lean in

**Repo:** no mention. The insurer's schedule lists the cruising area as
"Caribbean."
**Manna/GPT:** "Palisade Porter" as a song written during an accidental
expedition stop in Grand Marais.
**Merch:** the shirt lists GRAND MARAIS, MN with an asterisk reading
*UNEXPECTED.*

**DECISION:** Canon. Grand Marais, Minnesota, on Lake Superior, is a
documented port of call of a Caribbean recording expedition.

The Archive states this flatly, offers no route, and does not speculate. The
distance and the required transit are not addressed, because addressing them
would mean proposing an explanation, and the Archive does not have one. When
asked, the Archive's position is that the evidence for Grand Marais is better
than the evidence for several ports nobody disputes.

This is the best unexplained fact available to the project. It is
technically possible — a vessel can reach Lake Superior by sea — which keeps
it inside the no-supernatural rule while remaining completely absurd. Do not
explain it. Do not have a character note that it is strange.

**Consequences:**
- Carries the 1981 evidence (A1, B1).
- Supports "Palisade Porter" (A7). Palisade Head is on that shore.
- `content/expedition/the-voyage.md` needs the ports of call and the
  Caribbean-only implication softened.

**Files:** `content/expedition/the-voyage.md`, new artifact record, `SEED.md`.

establishing what the phrase is.

the Archive, while **the site's own furniture stays institutional.** The
Archive displays the seal. The Archive is not designed like the seal.

## A11. Ports of call — the shirt is now a primary document

**Source:** the concept shirt reverse, supplied 2026-08-02.

This was not on the agenda and it is the largest single injection of new
canon in this reconciliation. The shirt prints fifteen ports:

> Fort Lauderdale FL · Nassau, Bahamas · San Juan, Puerto Rico · Aruba ·
> Cartagena, Colombia · Panama City, Panama · Isla Colón · Gulf of Papagayo ·
> Acapulco, Mexico · Cabo San Lucas, Mexico · Mazatlán, Mexico · Isla Mujeres,
> Mexico · Cozumel, Mexico · Key West, FL · Grand Marais, MN\*
> *\*UNEXPECTED*

**DECISION:** Adopt as canon, with the following observations recorded rather
than smoothed over.

**a. The route requires two Panama Canal transits.** Panama City and the
Gulf of Papagayo and the Mexican Pacific ports are on one side; Isla Colón,
Cartagena, Isla Mujeres and Cozumel are on the other. The Archive should
state the ports and not the route. No transit records have been located,
which is itself worth one dry sentence.

**b. Isla Colón is Bocas del Toro.** The blender was recovered in 1988 off
Bocas del Toro. The shirt independently places the Expedition there. These
corroborate, and the corroboration should be noted flatly in the blender's
record. Free consistency; take it.

**c. Cabo San Lucas is on the list.** The current Cabo Fever entry says the
charter logs "do not record the ketch ever visiting." Both stay. The charter
logs do not record it; the commemorative shirt does. The Archive holds both
documents and finds the shirt the weaker authority while conceding it is the
more specific one. This is a better version of the existing joke, arrived at
by accident.

**d. The shirt's voice is looser than the Archive's.** "1 QUESTIONABLE
DENTIST" and "1 REALLY GOOD BLENDER" are jokier than museum-placard register,
and "questionable" is not "unlicensed." This is not a problem once A13 lands:
**the shirt is a Restoration Initiative fundraising item, not an Archive
publication.** The Initiative is allowed a warmer voice. The Archive is not.
The two-institution model earns its keep immediately here.

**Files:** `content/expedition/the-voyage.md`,
`content/artifacts/the-blender.md`, `content/merch/products.md`, `SEED.md`.

## A13. The Restoration Initiative — adopt

**Repo:** does not exist. The rights dispute in `content/notices/` ends with

**Manna/GPT:** the full institutional resolution.

**DECISION:** Adopt. This is the highest-value merge available and it closes

**The structure:**
- **Cayo Smooth Expedition Archive** — prior discovery, provenance, custody

- **Cayo Smooth Restoration Initiative** — the modern conservation and

  humidity.

> Published by the Cayo Smooth Restoration Initiative
> *By arrangement with the Cayo Smooth Expedition Archive.*

**Adopted positions from the source correspondence:** the Initiative's role
is to "gently remove the mildew"; it seeks no ownership of the source
conceit; **if evidence identifying Terry appears, the Initiative may misfile

section for the Initiative itself.

**What this solves beyond the joke:** it is how three real people appear in
the project without pretending to be the band. Modern restoration staff, 2026
layer, no period costume, no likeness problem.

**Deferred:** public job titles. "Chris Manna, Assistant Chief Restoration

# Part B — New questions these decisions created

## B1. What is the Grand Marais evidence?

A1 and A6 require a physical object, recovered at or traceable to Grand
Marais, dated 1981, that the Archive cannot dismiss. It becomes CSE-A-005 and
it is the load-bearing object of the date dispute.

**Requirements.** Mundane. Bureaucratic. Correctly filled out. Its power must
come from being in order, not from being strange — if it is strange it can be
dismissed, and the dispute collapses.

**RECOMMENDATION — a transient dockage receipt.** A Grand Marais marina slip
receipt, 1981, for a vessel of fifty-two feet. Paid. Signed illegibly. The
Archive's difficulty is that the paperwork is unremarkable in every respect
except that the ketch was gone by then and the vessel length matches exactly.

**Alternatives:** a dated hardware invoice; a photograph with a legible date
stamp and no legible subject. The receipt is better — an image invites
interpretation, and a receipt only invites arithmetic.

**Needs:** Zach's ruling.

## B2. Which stub dies?

A7 recommends #40 "Freeboard." Needs a nod, or a substitution.

## B3. What was the Cabo episode not?

A4 removes the illness. The replacement is deliberately empty. Confirm that
nobody wants it filled — the recommendation is that it stays empty
permanently and joins the protected mysteries list alongside Terry.

## B4. Does the modern layer appear on the site?

A13 establishes the Restoration Initiative. Separate question: does the
public site show 2026 restoration staff, or does the Initiative exist only in
the notices and the publication credit? This affects information
architecture and should be decided before the site grows.

## B5. Vocalist attribution — how visible?

A3 establishes rotating vocalists. Open: whether released-track frontmatter
names specific one-off vocalists, or whether attribution stays deliberately
incomplete in most cases. **Recommendation: mostly incomplete.** Name two or
three across sixty-one tracks and leave the rest blank or contested.
