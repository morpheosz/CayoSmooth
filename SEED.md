# Cayo Smooth Expedition — Seed Document

**Purpose.** A single self-contained brief for generative tools — image models,
video, audio, copy. It carries the full premise, the voice rules, the hard
canon, and physical descriptions of every object, so that anything produced
elsewhere lands consistent with the archive.

**How to read it.** Sections 1–3 are instructions, written plainly and out of
character. Sections 4 onward are source material, written in the archive's
own voice; quote and extend that voice, don't paraphrase it into something
breezier.

**Status.** Reflects the site as published. Not linked from the site's
navigation.

---

## 1. Premise in one paragraph

The Cayo Smooth Expedition Archive is a fictional institution documenting a
fictional event, presented entirely straight, as though it were a real
historical record. In 1979 an A&R man named Gil Vandermeer chartered a
fifty-two-foot ketch, loaded it with a Fender Rhodes, four studio musicians,
an unlicensed dentist, and roughly nine hundred pounds of provisions legally
classified as "cheese," and sailed for what he described in a telegram as
"the smooth latitudes." The expedition was gone fourteen months and returned
with sixty-one recorded songs and no ketch. The tapes were recovered eleven
years later from a Panamanian duty-free warehouse, in a crate mislabelled
DENTAL EQUIPMENT — FRAGILE — DO NOT PLAY. The archive catalogues the
recordings, the surviving objects, and the people, and it is defensive about
its claim to all of them.

The subject is yacht rock. The treatment is a museum.

---

## 2. Voice — the single most important constraint

**Play it completely straight.** The archive is a serious institution
documenting a real expedition. It does not know it is funny. It has never
knowingly made a joke. The humour lives entirely in the gap between the
deadpan institutional register and the absurd subject matter. Every wink at
the reader closes that gap and kills it.

**Register.** Museum placard, maritime insurance adjuster, or slightly
defensive academic. Short declarative sentences. Concrete nouns. Specific
numbers, especially unnecessary ones.

- Right: "The blender was recovered in 1988 off Bocas del Toro and is
  currently on loan to a museum."
- Wrong: "LOL and then they found the blender, which is honestly the funniest
  part."

**What works**

- Bureaucratic understatement — alarming facts stated as routine
- Unnecessary precision — "between 82 and 84 BPM"
- Unresolved gaps — Terry is never identified
- Institutional defensiveness — the archive gets testy when questioned
- The escalating footnote — a casual detail later referenced as long settled

**What does not work**

- Exclamation points
- Explaining the joke in the following sentence
- Modern internet cadence ("literally," "chef's kiss," "not gonna lie")
- Any character being aware they are in a bit
- Puns as punchlines. Puns as *song titles* are encouraged.

### 2.1 Two institutions, two registers

**The Archive** holds discovery, provenance, and custody. It speaks in the
register above. The expedition record, the artifacts, the personnel files,
and the discography are all the Archive.

**The Restoration Initiative** is the modern conservation and publication
body, working by arrangement with the Archive. It is warmer and a half-step
looser — a 2026 institution writing publication and promotional copy. It may
be plainly enthusiastic. It still never explains a joke and never
acknowledges that the Expedition is absurd. Merchandise and release material
are the Initiative.

This is why a commemorative shirt reads "1 REALLY GOOD BLENDER" while the
artifact record reads "CSE-A-001." Both are correct. They are different
institutions and they occasionally contradict each other in public.

When in doubt, write as the Archive.

**Rule of thumb.** When in doubt, understate, and cut the last sentence if it
explains the joke.

---

## 3. Hard canon — do not contradict

- The ketch is gone. It does not come back.
- Terry is unidentified. Terry stays unidentified.
- The blender is real, recovered, on loan, and has had no condition report
  since 2019.
- "Cabo Fever (Reprise)" has no original version and never did.
- Every track is in a major seventh. Tempos are 82–84 BPM.
- Sixty-one tracks total. Five released: the four founding releases plus
  "Anchorage," cleared later as the closer. New releases should be rare and
  deliberate.
- Nothing is 100% intact. Dilution is the medium.
- The Archive dates the return to 1980. One object, recovered at Grand Marais
  and dated 1981, contradicts this and has not been dismissed. This is the
  Archive's most contested fact and it stays unresolved in both directions.
- Grand Marais, Minnesota is a recorded port of call. No route is given, no
  explanation is offered, and none is ever attempted.
- Vandermeer is the most frequently credited vocalist, not the only one.
  Vocalists rotated; some were engaged at ports to settle debts ashore.
  Attribution is incomplete and stays that way. Port vocalists get credits,
  never dossiers.
- The Cabo episode is referenced and never described. It is not an illness.
- Two institutions, as described in §2.1. Releases carry: *Published by the
  Cayo Smooth Restoration Initiative, by arrangement with the Cayo Smooth
  Expedition Archive.*

Anything that would contradict the above must be rejected, or reconciled
in-universe — as a correction the archive issues about itself — never
silently retconned.

## 4. Founding document (full text)

> **Field Report: The Cayo Smooth Expedition**
>
> The tapes were not, as legend insisted, buried in a Malibu storage unit.
> They were in a Panamanian duty-free warehouse, in a crate mislabeled
> "DENTAL EQUIPMENT — FRAGILE — DO NOT PLAY."
>
> Our team had been chasing the rumor for eleven years. A retired session
> bassist named Duane "The Cushion" Petrosky mentioned it once at a boat show
> in Fort Lauderdale, allegedly while eating shrimp with both hands: that in
> 1979, an A&R man named Gil Vandermeer chartered a fifty-two-foot ketch,
> loaded it with a Fender Rhodes, four studio musicians, an unlicensed
> dentist, and roughly nine hundred pounds of provisions that were legally
> classified as "cheese," and set sail for what he described in a telegram as
> "the smooth latitudes."
>
> They were gone for fourteen months. They came back with sixty-one songs and
> no ketch.
>
> The recordings are, by any measure, the deepest yacht rock ever committed to
> tape. Not merely nautical — *submerged*. The tempos hover between 82 and 84
> BPM with a consistency that our lab initially attributed to equipment
> failure. Every track is in a major seventh. Several are in two major
> sevenths simultaneously, which our theorist called "not possible" and then
> requested to hear again.
>
> Highlights from the trove:
>
> - **"Marina, Don't"** — a six-minute plea addressed, we believe, to a marina.
> - **"Chardonnay Diplomacy"** — features a saxophone solo that begins at 2:14
>   and does not conclude within the runtime of the song.
> - **"(You're the) Second Mate of My Heart"** — nine verses, no chorus,
>   extremely apologetic.
> - **"Cabo Fever (Reprise)"** — there is no original version. There has never
>   been an original version. The reprise is all that exists and it refers
>   confidently to events not depicted.
>
> The most striking artifact is a fourteen-minute closer titled "Anchorage,"
> which is not about Alaska and is instead about anchoring. Midway through, the
> vocalist stops singing and simply lists boat parts in a warm, reassuring
> baritone for ninety seconds. Two members of our restoration team fell asleep.
> One of them woke up believing he owned a boat.
>
> Provenance remains murky. Vandermeer's own account, recovered from a
> water-damaged journal, is unhelpful; the final entry reads only, "Reached the
> smoothness. It goes further. Sending Terry back with the tapes and the good
> blender." Terry was never identified. The blender was recovered in 1988 off
> Bocas del Toro and is currently on loan to a museum.
>
> We have released four tracks. The remaining fifty-seven are being introduced
> to the public gradually, on the advice of a panel of cardiologists.
>
> Play responsibly. Do not operate a vessel while listening to side B.

## 5. The rights dispute (full text — the best voice sample)

A second party began independent development of the same material. The
resulting exchange establishes the archive's institutional register:
defensive, procedural, faintly aggrieved. The fourth document establishes the
Initiative's, which is the same posture from the other side of the table.

### 5.1 Notice of Prior Discovery and Licensing Availability

> Issued by the Cayo Smooth Expedition Archive (est. this afternoon)
>
> It has come to our attention that a second party has begun independent
> excavation of the Vandermeer materials. We welcome scholarly interest. We
> note, however, that this Archive holds originating rights to the Expedition,
> the ketch, the cheese manifest, the blender, and Terry.
>
> Our claim rests on the following: we found the crate. We opened the crate. We
> listened to side B and one of us woke up believing he owned a boat. This is
> the recognized standard of provenance in our field.
>
> We do not wish to be adversarial. We wish to be smooth. To that end, we are
> prepared to negotiate a licensing arrangement on generous terms, including
> but not limited to: use of the Expedition name, the sixty-one recovered
> tracks, the phrase "the smooth latitudes," and any saxophone solo that has
> not yet concluded.
>
> Standard rate is one (1) frozen drink, redeemable at a marina of our
> choosing. Volume discounts available. Sublicensing of Terry requires separate
> discussion.
>
> We look forward to a productive dialogue. Our counsel is on a boat and will
> respond when the wind changes.

### 5.2 Counterparty assurance (received)

> "Please inform them that NONE of his (hers?) initial inspiration has been
> modified or diluted! 100% intact"

### 5.3 Response to Counterparty Assurance

> Filed by the Cayo Smooth Expedition Archive
>
> We acknowledge receipt of the representative's assurance that the source
> material remains 100% intact, undiluted, and unmodified. We appreciate the
> sentiment. We must, however, register a concern.
>
> Nothing about this Expedition is 100% intact. The ketch is gone. The journal
> is water-damaged. "Cabo Fever (Reprise)" refers confidently to events that
> were never recorded and possibly never occurred. Terry has not been
> identified. The blender is on loan and we have not received a condition
> report since 2019.
>
> Dilution is not a defect in this field. It is the medium. The material
> arrived diluted. Vandermeer diluted it himself, at sea, on purpose, in a
> blender.
>
> We therefore ask the counterparty to reconsider the framing. An assurance of
> total preservation raises questions we are not equipped to answer, chiefly:
> preserved from what, and by whom, and were they on the boat.
>
> That said, we remain open to license. Our position is unchanged and our terms
> are generous. We would simply prefer the phrase "substantially intact,
> allowing for evaporation."
>
> Our counsel is still on the boat. The wind has not changed.

### 5.4 Reply of the Restoration Initiative

The counterparty resolves into an institution and accepts every term.

> We accept both, including the correction, which was deserved. […] We make no
> claim. The Archive found the crate. […] We are not here to rewrite the
> Expedition. We are here to gently remove the mildew.
>
> **On Terry.** We have no intention of identifying Terry. […] Should material
> come into our possession that appears to identify Terry, our position is
> that it will be misfiled. We consider this the responsible course and we are
> prepared to defend it.
>
> **On the blender.** We note with concern that the blender is the most
> thoroughly documented object associated with the Expedition. We do not know
> what to make of this. We raise it because we assume the Archive has also
> noticed and has also declined to raise it.
>
> We understand that the Archive's counsel remains on a boat. We are content
> to wait, and we have taken the liberty of assuming that the wind will change.

**Phrases worth reusing:** "the smooth latitudes"; "substantially intact,
allowing for evaporation"; "dilution is the medium"; "we wish to be smooth";
"our counsel is on a boat and will respond when the wind changes"; "it goes
further"; "gently remove the mildew"; "it will be misfiled."

## 6. The expedition

**The voyage.** Departed Fort Lauderdale, 1979. The charter was for six weeks.
The expedition returned fourteen months later without the vessel, by means the
archive has not established. Provisions: roughly nine hundred pounds under a
customs classification of "cheese," accepted at the time and never formally
revisited. The archive has reviewed the manifest and can confirm that some of
it was cheese. Course was given by telegram as "the smooth latitudes," with no
bearing filed; the insurer's schedule lists the cruising area as "Caribbean,"
which was broad enough to have been accurate and is now known not to have
been. Recording was continuous.

There is no distress call on file, no insurance claim, no salvage report, and
no journal entry marking the loss of the ketch. The vessel is present in the
early material by implication and absent at the end by implication. Between
those two states the record contains nothing. The archive does not know what
happened to the ketch.

**The ports.** The charter log stops recording calls after the eleventh week.
The archive has compiled the ports from harbour dues, fuel receipts, and
provisioning invoices — a secondary reconstruction, identified as one wherever
used. Fifteen calls are documented:

> Fort Lauderdale · Nassau · San Juan · Aruba · Cartagena · Panama City ·
> Isla Colón · Gulf of Papagayo · Acapulco · Cabo San Lucas · Mazatlán ·
> Isla Mujeres · Cozumel · Key West · Grand Marais

Six lie on the Pacific side of the Isthmus and eight on the Atlantic, which
requires two transits of the Panama Canal. No transit records have been
located. The Canal authority advised in 1997 that records of the period are
available on request; the archive has requested them four times.

The fifteenth is Grand Marais, Minnesota, on Lake Superior. Three documents
place the expedition there: a fuel receipt, a provisioning invoice, and a
dockage receipt (CSE-A-005). The archive has no route by which the ketch
reached Lake Superior and has not proposed one. It observes only that the
documentation for Grand Marais is more substantial than the documentation for
Aruba, which no one disputes.

**The return date.** The archive dates the return to 1980 and has done so
since 1992. The charter, the insurer's schedule, the ledger's final entry (a
chandlery in Key West, 1980), and a tape stock discontinued in 1980 all agree.
Petrosky, asked four times, gave three different years, none of them 1981.

Against this stands the dockage receipt: one vessel, fifty-two feet, ketch
rig, eleven nights, paid, dated 1981. It is in order in every respect,
including a matching duplicate stub recovered separately in 2016. The archive
has not revised the date and has not stopped arguing about it. Both positions
are held simultaneously and have been for some years.

**The smooth latitudes.** Vandermeer's phrase, first in the departure telegram
as the destination, then eleven times in the journal, always undefined. The
archive treats it as a place name — a cataloguing decision, not a geographic
claim. No coordinates were filed. Four positions have been proposed by
inference, no two within six hundred nautical miles of each other, all in open
water, none visited. The journal's final legible entry is the only substantive
statement: "Reached the smoothness. It goes further."

**The recovery.** Eleven years, from the boat show to the warehouse. The rumour
placed the tapes in a storage unit in Malibu; the archive pursued it for six
years. The unit exists. It contains, and contained throughout, patio furniture.
The correction came from Petrosky, who had mentioned Panama before — the
difference on that occasion was that someone wrote it down. The warehouse was
located in 1990. Crate 14 was in the second row. Baking and transfer took two
years. Four tracks were prepared for release.

## 7. Personnel

**Gil Vandermeer** — A&R; charter principal; vocals. Credited vocalist on
thirty-eight of the sixty-one tracks — more than any other party, and well
short of all of them. Professional record before 1979 is thin and unremarkable,
which the archive has come to regard as the most interesting thing about the
file. Did not return. The archive receives roughly two letters a year proposing
he is alive, answers all of them, and has never been persuaded.

**Duane "The Cushion" Petrosky** — bass, backing vocals; credited on all
sixty-one tracks. The archive's origin point: mentioned the tapes once at a
Fort Lauderdale boat show, allegedly while eating shrimp with both hands. The
shrimp detail appears in every retelling and in no signed statement. Cooperative
afterwards and imprecise — gave three different years across four
conversations, none of them 1979. On Terry he was consistent and unhelpful.
Returned. The bass has not been located.

**Dr. Milton Fauve** — dentist (unlicensed); spoken word. Licence lapsed 1976,
never renewed. The manifest lists him as "Dr. Fauve, dentist," which is
two-thirds accurate. No record of any dental procedure during the fourteen
months, and no record of one being required. Credited by the session log with
the inventory passage in "Anchorage"; the tape box credits Vandermeer. Was
never paid. Returned.

**Terry** — role unknown, status unidentified. Referenced exactly once, in the
journal's final entry: "Sending Terry back with the tapes and the good
blender." Appears in no manifest, ledger, session log, or tape box. Eleven
candidate identifications pursued since 1991: three withdrawn, six eliminated
on documentary grounds, two eliminated because the candidate, when contacted,
was not Terry. **Terry must never be identified, described physically, or
depicted.**

**The roster (disputed).** Charter, insurer, and ledger all record four studio
musicians: Lonnie Ashe (Fender Rhodes, all 61), Petrosky (bass, all 61), Ray
Kettering (drums, all 61), Sonny Delgado (saxophone, 9). The archive's own
catalogue entry for "Chardonnay Diplomacy" describes the roster as three names
plus Delgado's, implying he was an addition rather than a member. That entry has
not been amended; the archive does not revise its process retroactively. Ashe
and Kettering returned and have never given an interview. Delgado returned. The
saxophone solo did not.

**Vocal attribution.** The expedition carried no singer. It carried a producer
who sang, four musicians who sometimes sang, and a standing arrangement for
engaging vocalists in port. The ledger books these fourteen times under a
category the bookkeeper entered as "settled ashore" — small sums, date and
port given, payee not. The inference, labelled as inference, is that vocal
engagements discharged debts incurred ashore: bar accounts, dockage, and in
two cases something the ledger calls "the other matter."

Fourteen payments against seven tape-box credits. Unreconciled.

Distribution as catalogued: Vandermeer 38, Petrosky 4, Ashe 1, port
engagements 7, no credit recorded 11.

Of the six named port singers, five appear in no other document anywhere. Two
exceptions. **R. Solís**, credited at Mazatlán, recurs eleven tracks later in
the same hand — a second engagement or a copying error, indistinguishable.
**Esperanza**, port unstated, sits against the liner notes to "Marina, Don't,"
which name the subject "Marina Esperanza," a facility the port authority has
never recorded. The archive notes the recurrence and declines to conclude
anything from it. The seventh credit is not a name; the box reads
`GUEST (PAPAGAYO)`.

The archive has declined to assign the eleven uncredited tracks by ear, on the
grounds that a credit assigned by ear is an opinion, in a field where the
archive has already been wrong twice.

## 8. Artifacts — physical description for depiction

Five accessions. Three held, one on loan, one unaccounted for.

### CSE-A-001 — The Blender *(on loan)*

Late-1970s four-speed household blender. Chrome collar between motor housing
and carafe. Glass carafe with moulded graduations up the side. Cream or
off-white enamelled motor housing, squat and tapered, with four rectangular
push-button speed controls in a row across the front. **The motor housing is
pitted along the base** — salt-water immersion. The carafe is original; this
has been questioned; the archive's position is that the carafe is original.

Recovered 1988 off Bocas del Toro by a salvage crew working an unrelated
contract. Identified from the journal's reference to "the good blender," which
establishes by implication that there was at least one other. The second
blender has not been recovered; the archive does not consider it lost, only
unrecovered. Bocas del Toro is Isla Colón, which the reconstructed port record
independently lists as a call — two documents compiled twenty-two years apart
that agree, which the archive records because it has few opportunities to.

On loan to a museum since 1991 under terms requiring an annual condition
report. The most recent report on file is dated 2019. The archive has written
four times and is confident the blender is fine.

### CSE-A-002 — Vandermeer's Journal *(held)*

Bound notebook, marbled cover, cloth spine, approximately 180 leaves, of which
44 are legible in full and 19 in part. Marbling is a combed pattern in browns
and tans. **Water damage across the lower portion** — tide lines, cockling,
staining that carries onto the fore-edge of the page block. The damage is
uniform across the volume, inconsistent with a single immersion event, and has
never been satisfactorily explained.

Entries are undated. Roughly a third concern weather, a further third
provisioning in detail the archive has elected not to reproduce, the remainder
smoothness and hard to follow. Digitised 2004; scans held offsite.

### CSE-A-003 — Crate 14 *(held)*

Wooden shipping crate, 118 × 61 × 58 cm — wide and low. Pine boards with
corner battens. Stencilled on two sides in plain black uppercase:

> DENTAL EQUIPMENT — FRAGILE — DO NOT PLAY

The third instruction is the significant one. It is not standard on dental
freight, and it is stencilled at the same weight as the first two. Whoever
crated the tapes knew what was in the crate, knew it could be played, and took
a position on whether it should be. Warehouse records give the consignor as
illegible and the consignee as "HOLD." Held under duty-free status for an
unbroken period, so no duty was assessed and no inspection required. No storage
fees were ever billed and none were ever paid. Retained empty, in the condition
in which it was found. It is not on loan and has never been on loan.

### CSE-A-004 — The Fender Rhodes *(unaccounted for)*

Seventy-three-key electric piano — 43 white keys, 30 black, spanning E to E.
Black tolex-covered case, chrome hardware, on a tubular stand. Audible on all
sixty-one tracks.

It did not come back. Neither did the ketch. The archive notes these are two
separate facts and asks that they not be combined into an assumption. It is
catalogued as an artifact rather than a loss on the grounds that the instrument
is present in the recordings, continuously, for fourteen months — a holding is
a holding. Tuning drifts flat across the trove by approximately a quarter tone,
consistent with humidity and with time. It does not drift evenly: on four
tracks it is sharp, and those four are not sequential and share no other
identified property. Six people have claimed to have seen it after 1980. Two
accounts place it in Panama. One places it in a hotel bar in Sarasota. The
archive has not travelled to Sarasota.

### CSE-A-005 — The Dockage Receipt *(held)*

Transient slip receipt, one leaf, 108 × 178 mm, printed in two colours on card
stock consistent with commercial job printing of the period. **Perforated
along the upper edge**, removed from a duplicate book. **Folded twice,
horizontally, and carried folded for some time** — the creases are soft and
the ink has offset slightly along the upper fold. Printed form gives a marina
at Grand Marais, Minnesota. Completed in blue ballpoint, legible in every
field except the last.

Vessel length 52 ft. Rig: ketch. Eleven nights. Rate itemised, including an
overlength surcharge. Paid in full, cash. Master of vessel: illegible. Dated
1981.

Deposited 2011 by a correspondent in Cook County, Minnesota, who found it in
his late father's marina paperwork. Under examination since, and nothing has
been found wrong with it. Stock, printing, and form layout are correct for the
marina and the period; the rate matches the schedule filed with the county;
the duplicate book was recovered separately in 2016 and contains the matching
stub. The signature has been compared against nine specimens of Vandermeer's.
It is illegible. It is not not his.

The single object standing against an otherwise consistent record, and the
reason the return date remains open.

## 9. Discography

Sixty-one tracks. Sequence follows the order in which the reels were boxed,
which the archive treats as authoritative in the absence of anything else. All
in a major seventh; all between 82 and 84 BPM.

Recordings are published by the Restoration Initiative in volumes —
*Recovered Recordings, Vol. I*, and onward — as restoration completes. Volume
membership is a publication decision and carries no implication about a
recording's place in the trove.

### Released

**1. "Marina, Don't"** — 6:04, E major seventh, 83 BPM, SLR-001. A six-minute
plea addressed directly, by name, to a marina, asking it repeatedly not to. The
liner notes identify it as "Marina Esperanza"; port authority records list no
marina of that name at any point between 1965 and the present. The song is
unaffected and continues to address it. Single extended verse, no bridge. The
Rhodes part does not resolve.

**2. "Chardonnay Diplomacy"** — 4:47, A major seventh, 84 BPM, SLR-002. A
negotiation, unspecified, resolved over wine; neither party nor terms are
named. The saxophone solo begins at 2:14 and the tape ends before it does, cut
mid-phrase by what the splice log records as a "reel change, unavoidable." No
second reel has been located. Played at official archive functions as a
processional — a recent policy, not yet tested against a processional shorter
than 4:47.

*This entry is pending amendment on transfer. See §12.*

**3. "(You're the) Second Mate of My Heart"** — 5:58, D major seventh, 82 BPM,
SLR-003. Nine verses, no chorus, extensively apologetic. The archive's
transcribed lyric sheet contains eight; the ninth is referenced in session notes
as "the one about the anchor windlass, cut for time," and its absence is treated
as confirmation that it was recorded. The apology is never specified.

**4. "Cabo Fever (Reprise)"** — 3:41, G major seventh, 84 BPM, SLR-004b. There
is no original version and never was. The catalogue reserved SLR-004a for a
recording that does not exist; the archive has declined to renumber, on the
grounds that renumbering would imply the gap needed explaining. Refers
throughout to an episode at Cabo which it does not describe and does not
introduce, with the confidence of a song recalling something the listener was
present for. What the episode was is recorded nowhere. The charter log does not
place the ketch at Cabo San Lucas; the reconstructed port record does; the
archive holds both and observes that a reprise of a nonexistent recording is
not evidence for either.

**61. "Anchorage"** — 14:02, C major seventh / D major seventh simultaneously,
82 BPM, SLR-061. The closer. Not about Alaska; concerns anchoring — technique,
etiquette, and, from roughly the six-minute mark, inventory. At 6:04 the vocal
stops and a second voice lists boat parts in a warm, reassuring baritone for
ninety seconds: bow roller, chain locker, snubber, swivel, the anchor itself by
three different names. Two of the restoration team fell asleep during a
supervised playback. One woke believing he owned a boat.

**For generated music.** 82–84 BPM, major seventh chords throughout, Fender
Rhodes forward in the mix and drifting a quarter tone flat, fretless-adjacent
bass, brushed or soft-stick drums, saxophone that overstays. Warm, submerged,
unhurried. Vocals conversational and apologetic rather than belted.

**On vocal variety.** Different singers across the trove is canon, not a
defect — see §7. A track may carry a voice heard nowhere else. It does not need
to sound like the same band every time; it needs to sound like the same room.

## 11. Visual direction

**The core instruction: resist "1978 album cover."** The stronger joke is that
the presentation looks like a real institutional archive — restrained serif
type, generous white space, hairline rules, small captions beneath object
photography — and the yacht rock is simply the subject matter being catalogued.
Sun-faded gradients, palm silhouettes, chrome script lettering, and cursive
neon all break the premise.

**The one exception: the expedition seal.** The seal is a 1979 artifact, and
period artifacts are allowed to look like period artifacts — warm, illustrative,
a ketch and a sun and the phrase set around the lower edge. The distinction is
layer, not taste. **The archive displays the seal. The archive is not designed
like the seal.** Merchandise, releases, and Initiative material may carry it;
the site's own furniture stays institutional.

**The seal.** The durable umbrella mark for the whole project — an expedition
insignia rather than a band logo. Reproduces on releases, shirts, hats,
manifests, tape boxes, stickers, and profile images, and survives one-colour
reproduction. Individual volumes may develop their own art direction without
detaching from it.

**Tagline.** `SEARCHING FOR THE SMOOTH LATITUDES`. Set around the seal's lower
edge, or standalone. Placement is deliberate; overuse turns it into decoration.

**Site palette.** Warm paper `#fbfaf7`, ink `#17191c`, muted grey-green
`#6b6e68`, hairline rules `#e2ded4`, deep navy accent `#2a4a63`. Dark theme
inverts to `#15171a` paper with `#e7e4dc` ink. Body type is an old-style serif
(Iowan Old Style / Palatino / Georgia). Labels are small caps, letterspaced.

**Merchandise palette.** Warmer than the site and period-correct: natural
cream ground, deep navy, teal, burnt orange. This is the seal's palette, not
the reading room's.

**Object photography.** Neutral seamless ground, soft even light, minimal or no
cast shadow, object centred and shot straight on or slightly above — the
conventions of a museum catalogue or an auction listing, not a lifestyle shoot.
Every object should read as *aged and used*: the blender pitted and dulled, the
journal cockled and stained, the crate scuffed and splintered at the battens,
the dockage receipt soft-creased and offset along the fold. Nothing pristine.
Nothing styled with props. No people, and no depiction of Terry under any
circumstance.

**Vandermeer.** Do not anchor his likeness to any real actor, celebrity, or
recognisable fictional character. Whether he is depicted at all is unresolved;
the working approach is elusive — silhouette, an empty chair, a hat on a chart
table, an obscured photograph, documents bearing his signature.

**Storefront photography.** The one place where "appealing product shot" and
"institutional plate" have to meet: the goods should look genuinely desirable
and physically real — correct materials, believable weight, honest fabric
texture and ceramic glaze — while still being lit and framed with the archive's
restraint. Think a well-made museum shop catalogue, not a vector mockup.
