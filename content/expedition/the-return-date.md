The Archive dates the return of the Expedition to 1980. It has dated the
return to 1980 since 1992. It expects to continue doing so.

## The case for 1980

The charter was executed in 1979 for a term of six weeks. The insurer's
schedule runs from 1979 and was not renewed. The ledger's final entry —
a payment to a chandlery, in Key West, for nothing more remarkable than
line and fuel — is dated 1980. Petrosky, asked directly on four separate
occasions, gave three different years, none of them 1979 and none of
them 1981.

The reels are the strongest evidence. Sixty-one masters were boxed in
sequence, the boxing hand does not change, and the tape stock across the
trove is of a single manufacturing lot. That lot was discontinued in
1980.

Fourteen months from a 1979 departure arrives in 1980. Every document
the Archive holds agrees, and the Archive holds a great many documents.

## The receipt

In 2011 a dockage receipt was deposited with the Archive by a
correspondent in Cook County, Minnesota, who had found it in a folder of
his late father's marina paperwork and thought it might be of interest.

It is a transient slip receipt from a marina at Grand Marais. It records
one vessel, length fifty-two feet, rigged as a ketch, for eleven nights.
It is paid in full. It is signed, illegibly, on the line for the master
of the vessel. It bears a date in 1981.

The Archive has examined it for thirteen years. The paper is correct for
the period. The printing is correct for the marina, which was in business
under that name and at that address. The dockage rate is consistent with
what the marina charged that season, including the surcharge for
overlength vessels, which is itemised. The marina's own duplicate book,
recovered separately in 2016, contains the matching stub.

There is nothing wrong with it.

## The difficulty

If the receipt is what it appears to be, then in 1981 someone paid eleven
nights of dockage at Grand Marais, Minnesota, for a fifty-two-foot ketch,
a year after the Expedition had returned without one.

The Archive has considered the available explanations and finds each of
them worse than the problem. That a different fifty-two-foot ketch called
at Grand Marais in 1981 is possible and would be the simplest answer, and
the Archive would accept it, except that no such vessel appears in the
harbourmaster's returns for that season and the Expedition's other two
Grand Marais documents are not in dispute. That the date is a clerical
error requires the same error in the duplicate stub. That the receipt is
a forgery requires a forger who obtained period paper, the correct
overlength surcharge, and a marina's duplicate book, and then waited
thirty years to produce a document that embarrasses a small archive and
enriches no one.

The Archive has not revised the date of return. It has also not stopped
arguing about it. The position of the reading room, stated here so that
correspondents need not write in to establish it, is that the Archive
dates the return to 1980 and holds one piece of paper that says
otherwise, and that both of those things are true at the same time and
have been for some years.

Enquiries proposing a fourteenth explanation are answered. The Archive
has answered all of them.
