Eleven years, from the boat show to the warehouse.

The rumour placed the tapes in a storage unit in Malibu. The Archive
pursued the storage unit for the first six of those years. The storage
unit exists. It contains, and contained throughout, patio furniture.

The correction came from the same source as the original rumour.
Petrosky, in conversation, referred to Panama. He had referred to Panama
before. The difference on this occasion was that someone wrote it down.

The warehouse was located in 1990. It held goods under duty-free status,
meaning no duty had been assessed and no inspection had ever been
required. Crate 14 was in the second row. It was stencilled DENTAL
EQUIPMENT — FRAGILE — DO NOT PLAY. Warehouse records gave the consignor
as illegible and the consignee as HOLD. No storage fees had been billed
in eleven years and none had been paid.

The crate contained sixty-one master reels in original boxes, in good
order. Baking and transfer were completed over the following two years.
Four tracks were prepared for release.

The Archive's restoration log records that the first supervised playback
of "Anchorage" was attended by six staff. Two fell asleep during the
inventory passage at 6:04. One woke up believing he owned a boat. He was
corrected, and the correction is minuted, and he has since retired.

This sequence — found the crate, opened the crate, listened to side B —
constitutes the Archive's claim of originating rights. It is the
recognised standard of provenance in this field.
