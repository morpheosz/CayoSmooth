The ketch departed Fort Lauderdale in 1979. The charter was for six
weeks. The Expedition returned fourteen months later, without the vessel,
by means the Archive has not established.

What is documented:

**The provisioning.** Roughly nine hundred pounds of provisions were
loaded under a customs classification of "cheese." The classification was
accepted at the time and has never been formally revisited. The Archive
has reviewed the manifest and can confirm that some of it was cheese.

**The instrument.** A Fender Rhodes, seventy-three keys, loaded at the
dock. Its presence on all sixty-one recordings establishes that it was
aboard for the full duration and that it worked.

**The course.** Given by telegram as "the smooth latitudes." No bearing
was filed. The insurer's schedule lists the cruising area as "Caribbean,"
which is broad enough to have been accurate.

**The recording.** Continuous. Sixty-one tracks, tempos between 82 and 84
BPM, every one in a major seventh. The consistency is the Expedition's
defining physical fact and the Archive's laboratory initially attributed
it to a fault in the transport mechanism. The transport mechanism was
subsequently tested and found sound.

What is not documented is the loss of the ketch. There is no distress
call on file, no insurance claim, no salvage report, and no entry in the
journal marking the event. The vessel is present in the early material by
implication and absent at the end by implication. Between those two
states the record contains nothing.

The Archive has been asked what happened to the ketch. The Archive does
not know what happened to the ketch.
