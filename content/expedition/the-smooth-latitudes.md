The phrase originates with Vandermeer and appears first in the departure
telegram, where it is given as the Expedition's destination. It appears
subsequently in the journal, eleven times, always without definition.

The Archive treats the phrase as a place name. This is a cataloguing
decision and should not be read as a geographic claim.

No coordinates were ever filed. Attempts to locate the smooth latitudes
by inference — from the recordings' consistency, from the provisioning,
from the drift of the Rhodes — have produced four proposed positions, no
two of which are within six hundred nautical miles of each other. All
four are in open water. None has been visited by the Archive.

The journal's final legible entry contains the Expedition's only
substantive statement on the matter:

> Reached the smoothness. It goes further.

The Archive notes that this sentence describes arrival and continuation
in the same breath, and that the second clause is the one Vandermeer
chose to end on. Whether "further" denotes distance, depth, or degree is
not recoverable from the text. Scholarship has generally assumed
distance. The Archive has no position.
