The closer. Not about Alaska. Concerns, at length, the act of anchoring:
technique, etiquette, and — for a period beginning at roughly the six-
minute mark — inventory.

At 6:04 the vocal stops and a second voice, uncredited on the original
tape box, lists boat parts in a warm, reassuring baritone for ninety
seconds: bow roller, chain locker, snubber, swivel, the anchor itself,
by three different names. The Archive's session log attributes this
passage to Dr. Milton Fauve, the Expedition's physician of record, who
held no formal musical credential and was aboard in a dental capacity.
The tape box attributes it to Vandermeer. Both documents are considered
authoritative. The Archive has not been asked to choose between them and
would prefer not to.

Two members of the restoration team fell asleep during a supervised
playback of this passage. One woke up believing he owned a boat. This is
recorded in the restoration log without further comment, which the
Archive considers the correct amount of comment.

This is one of the tracks the Expedition's theorist described as being in
two major sevenths simultaneously, a claim he retracted, then repeated,
then declined to discuss further.

Cleared for release after the founding four. No further tracks are
scheduled for release at this time.
