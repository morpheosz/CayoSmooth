Wooden shipping crate, 118 × 61 × 58 cm, stencilled on two sides:

> DENTAL EQUIPMENT — FRAGILE — DO NOT PLAY

The third instruction is the one the Archive regards as significant. It
is not standard on dental freight. Whoever crated the tapes knew what was
in the crate, knew it could be played, and took a position on whether it
should be.

The crate held the sixty-one master reels. It was warehoused in Panama
under duty-free status for an unbroken period, meaning no duty was ever
assessed on it and no inspection was ever required. The Archive located
it in the eleventh year of searching. It was in the second row.

Warehouse records list the consignor as illegible and the consignee as
"HOLD." No storage fees were ever billed. No storage fees were ever paid.
Both facts are documented and neither is disputed.

The crate is retained in the Archive's possession, empty, in the
condition in which it was found. It is not on loan. The Archive wishes to
be clear that the crate is not on loan and has never been on loan.
