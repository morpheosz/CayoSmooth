The Cayo Smooth Restoration Initiative is the conservation and
publication body working the Expedition materials, by arrangement with
the Cayo Smooth Expedition Archive.

We did not find the tapes. That was the Archive, and the Archive has our
unqualified recognition for it. We came later, and we came for a narrower
reason: the reels were recovered in 1990 from a warehouse without climate
control, they had already been baked once, and magnetic tape of that age
does not wait politely while an institution decides what to do about it.

## What we do

**Transfer.** Reels are stabilised and transferred to formats less
susceptible to humidity than the ones they arrived in. Each transfer is
documented. Where a transfer reveals something the catalogue did not
know, the catalogue is amended and the amendment is published rather
than absorbed.

**Publication.** Recovered recordings are issued as volumes —
*Recovered Recordings, Vol. I*, and onward — as restoration completes.
The volumes are not a planned sequence. They are what was ready.
Membership in a volume is our decision and carries no implication about
the recording's place in the trove, which is fixed by the order the reels
were boxed in and is not ours to alter.

**Nothing else.** We do not add to the historical record. Where our
work produces a document — a transfer log, a condition note — it is
filed as a 2026 document and dated as one.

## What we do not do

We make no claim to the Expedition, the ketch, the cheese manifest, the
blender, or Terry. These are the Archive's.

We have no intention of identifying Terry. Should conservation work
produce material appearing to identify Terry, it will be misfiled. This
position is on the record. See *Reply of the Restoration Initiative*.

## Personnel

The Initiative is three people. Their names are on the transfer logs
where the transfer logs require names and nowhere else. They are not
Expedition members, they are not depicted in period dress, and they were
not born in time to have been on the boat.
